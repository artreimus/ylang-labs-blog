## Original illustrations and numeric checks

- The illustrative gradient-descent step is `1 − 0.1 × (−2) = 1.2`. A negative gradient gives a local direction; no measured improvement is claimed.
- `A B C D` is an illustrative token sequence. Its three prefix/next-token pairs are explanatory views of targets that can be processed in parallel under a causal mask.
- At a fixed hard target, cross-entropy is lower for probability 0.8 than 0.1. These are illustrative probabilities, not measured model outputs.
- Four GRPO rewards of `[1, 0, 1, 0]` have mean 0.5. The article describes above- and below-average signals without asserting a particular library’s normalization convention.
- 8,000,000,000 weights × 2 bytes = 16,000,000,000 bytes = 16 GB decimal, for weights only. This is not a total training-memory estimate.
- The article reports no measured training improvements, benchmark gains, hardware costs, or original model experiments.

## Technical boundaries preserved

- Neural networks require nonlinear operations to go beyond a composition of affine maps. Backpropagation computes local derivatives; an update does not guarantee better held-out behavior.
- Token IDs are labels, while embeddings are learned numerical representations. The observed continuation is a target, not a guarantee of truth or the only valid continuation.
- Causal masking prevents future-target leakage. Ordinary autoregressive inference uses trained parameters without updating them.
- SFT response-only loss is described as common, not universal. Learned-reward RLHF and original offline DPO are different procedures.
- GRPO updates shared parameters from relative feedback. Equal-reward groups have no within-group reward-relative signal; other objective terms can still update the model.
- Whole-answer rewards do not validate every reasoning step. Reasoning can also be elicited by prompting or learned through SFT and distillation.
- Data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism divide different work. State sharding is an additional memory technique.
- LoRA freezes base weights and learns an additive two-factor adjustment. QLoRA’s four-bit description concerns frozen base-weight storage, not all computation or all training state.
- Training reward, generalization, correctness, and novelty require different evidence. No source or review establishes universal factual accuracy.
